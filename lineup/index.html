i
Who starts is always decided by rank first. Turn this off if you'd rather FLEX just reflect rank order with no extra slot-shuffling.

My Lineup Strategist Guide

Welcome to My Lineup Strategist! This tool is designed to eliminate start/sit headaches and bring clarity to your weekly fantasy football lineup decisions.

1 Syncing & Adding Leagues

Navigate to the Dashboard tab and enter your Sleeper Username and League ID to sync your roster automatically. You can manage multiple leagues and switch between them using the header dropdown or the League Command Center. If you don't use Sleeper, tap "Create Manual" and add players one-by-one.

2 Uploading Custom Rankings & SoS

Upload custom analyst rankings in either .CSV or native .XLSX Excel format.

Upload Rest-of-Season (ROS) rankings on the Roster tab, and Weekly Matchup rankings on the Lineup tab. You can also upload or manually input a Strength of Schedule (SoS) matrix in the Roster tab to instantly generate color-coded matchup gradients on player cards. Note: If you upload a generic rankings CSV that contains a 'Matchup' or 'SoS' column, the app will automatically extract it for you. If your file also has a Tier column, each player's tier is shown next to their rank on player cards, like (T2).

3 Lineup Optimizer & Early Games

The Lineup Optimizer generates your highest-projected starting lineup based on your Weekly Rankings. Lock foundational starters with or tap ⇄ Swap to manually override positions. Select teams playing in early windows (TNF, London) under Early Games to flag them with an EARLY badge across your lineup before roster locks. Each starter and bench player also shows their opponent and Sleeper's projection for the week (labeled proj); once that player's game is final, their actual score takes its place with the projection shown beneath it. These are for reference only - the optimizer still runs on your rankings - and they update when you open or interact with the tab, not live during games.

4 Matchup Simulator

Found on the Lineup tab, this runs 10,000 simulated matchups between your starting lineup and your current Sleeper opponent's, using each player's own week-to-week variance rather than just their season average. If you swap a player in the Lineup Optimizer before running it, the simulation reflects your in-app lineup - even if you haven't pushed that change to Sleeper yet - so you can test "what if I started X instead of Y" before committing to it.

Each player's expected score comes from whichever of these is available, in order: Sleeper's own weekly projection for this specific matchup (factors in opponent, injury designation, etc.), this season's actual scores, and last season's scores as a supplement when this season's sample is still small; last season's influence fades out as your current-season sample grows, rather than being used indefinitely.

Boom/Bust thresholds are fixed point totals, not relative to any one player's own average, so "boom" means the same real accomplishment whether it's your WR1 or your flex streamer:

  • QB: Boom 24+ pts  /  Bust <12 pts
  • RB: Boom 20+ pts  /  Bust <7 pts
  • WR: Boom 20+ pts  /  Bust <7.5 pts
  • TE: Boom 15+ pts  /  Bust <5.5 pts
  • K / DEF: no widely-used standard exists for these, so Boom/Bust is instead based on each player's own average (50%/150% of it).

A R badge flags rookies, and a ~ before a player's range means there wasn't enough game history yet to measure their variance directly; it's an early-season estimate, not a measured one. The Lineup Insights section (when shown) flags bench players projected to outscore the weakest starter they're eligible to replace.

This is meant for exploring possibilities, not as start/sit advice. It's built on historical data, projections, and estimated variance; it has no way to know about a role change, coaching change, or opportunity shift that hasn't shown up in the data yet.

5 Scout & Waivers

The Scout tab is your interactive transaction hub. Paste waiver wire recommendations or trade targets. The app cross-references your Sleeper Sync data to show whether players are available on waivers, on your roster, or owned by opponents, alongside side-by-side Weekly and ROS ranks. The Waiver Wire Assistant's Auto-Find lists the top available players by your Weekly or ROS rankings, with each one's weekly positional and FLEX rank. Compare them against your Starting Lineup (would he start this week, and who would he replace?) or your Whole Roster (is he better than your weakest player at the position?). Pasted lists get the same Would Start check, and the Search In toggle above the paste box will run that same list against every league you have synced at once - one card per player showing where he is a free agent, where he is already on your roster, and who owns him in the rest, with a Switch button to jump straight to any of them. The Trade Analyzer tells you who owns each player and totals up each side's value using your own custom ROS Rankings from the Roster tab, and if you've also loaded Market Value data from the Trade Finder section, it shows a second verdict from market consensus for comparison. The values used for your ROS Rankings are estimated by converting your ROS Rank into a point value on a 0-10,000 scale, weighted so top-ranked players are worth disproportionately more; rank #1 player is around 10,000, and the values decay from there at ~1.8% per rank. It's a way to help compare players on your own board; not intended to be an exact evaluation of your rankings.

6 Trade Finder & Market Disconnects

Pull live market consensus values from FantasyCalc or LeagueLogs (or upload a market CSV). Use the Trade Finder to filter by flat rank gaps or percentage shifts to instantly uncover high-value buy-low targets and overvalued sell-high assets relative to your custom ROS board. Rank Basis controls what those gaps are measured against: Overall compares each player's rank across the whole player pool, while Positional Rank compares them only against others at their own position (QB vs QB, RB vs RB, etc.). Positional Rank is the better choice in Superflex or TE Premium leagues; those formats make market consensus price entire positions differently than a standard board would, which can otherwise flag a fairly-valued player (e.g. a top QB in a Superflex league) as a false buy-low or sell-high. One thing to know: positional gaps run much smaller than overall ones; a 10-spot Overall gap is a reasonable default, but a 10-spot gap within a single position is enormous, so lower the Minimum Rank Gap (2-4 is a good starting point) when you switch to Positional Rank.

7 Positional Power Rankings

Found on the Scout tab, this tool evaluates every team's positional strength based on your custom ROS rankings or live market data. It generates a heatmap table of your league's rosters—hover or tap on any positional rank to see the specific players driving that score. Use this to quickly find ideal trade partners who are strong where you are weak!

8 Exporting & Sharing

Once your lineup is finalized, tap the Export button to generate a clean, high-resolution screenshot of your Optimal Lineup to share with friends or post on social media!
